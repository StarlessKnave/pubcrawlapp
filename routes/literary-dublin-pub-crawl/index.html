Literary Dublin Pub Crawl

In the Footsteps of Joyce, Behan, Flann O'Brien & Kavanagh

Trace the footsteps of Ireland's Nobel laureates and bohemian wordsmiths. From Leopold Bloom's gorgonzola sandwich at Davy Byrnes to the journalistic debating rooms of The Palace Bar, this is Dublin's literary soul in pint form.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which pub on this route is featured in Ulysses? ▼

Davy Byrnes on Duke Street is famously featured in Chapter 8 (Lestrygonians) of James Joyce's Ulysses, where Leopold Bloom stops for a gorgonzola sandwich and a glass of burgundy.

2. Did James Joyce drink at The Palace Bar? ▼

While Joyce left Dublin in 1904, The Palace Bar became the primary salon for Dublin's mid-century literary renaissance under editor R.M. Smyllie, frequented by Flann O'Brien and Patrick Kavanagh.

3. Why is John Kavanagh's called 'The Gravediggers'? ▼

Built directly against the wall of Glasnevin Cemetery in 1833, gravediggers used to tap on the pub's exterior wall to order pints through an opening while working night shifts.

4. Is Toners connected to any famous writers? ▼

W.B. Yeats notoriously despised pubs, but agreed to visit Toners on Baggot Street with Oliver St. John Gogarty. After finishing his sherry, Yeats famously remarked, 'Now I have seen a pub.'
