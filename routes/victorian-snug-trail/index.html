Victorian Snug Trail

Hidden Wooden Snugs & 19th-Century Partitions

Step behind frosted stained glass into Dublin's surviving Victorian snugs. Originally built in the 1800s for discreet conversations, matchmaking, and parish business, these wooden booths remain the coziest sanctuaries in the city.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What exactly is a pub snug? ▼

A snug is a small, partitioned private room built inside 19th-century Irish pubs. It typically features frosted or stained glass, a dedicated door to the street, and a hatch window to order directly from the bartender.

2. Do snugs cost extra to sit in? ▼

Historically snugs carried a halfpenny surcharge per pint, but today they are free and open to all patrons on a first-come, first-served basis.

3. Which pub has Dublin's most iconic snug? ▼

Kehoe's on South Anne Street features Dublin's most famous front snug, unchanged with original mahogany paneling and service buzzer.

4. How many people fit inside a traditional snug? ▼

Most Victorian snugs seat between 4 and 8 people comfortably, making them ideal for small groups of friends.
