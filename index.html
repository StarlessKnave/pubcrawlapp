How the Stout Score is Assigned

Pint ratings, cellar standards & heritage benchmarks

The Stout Score is a curated qualitative index evaluating Dublin's best pints of plain. Rather than marketing claims, scores are compiled by cross-referencing traditional cellar infrastructure, certified pour standards, local connoisseurs, and Irish beer media.

The 6 Evaluation Pillars

1. Cellar Care & Short Lines (30%)

Short line distance from chilled cellar kegs to the tap at regulated 6°C preserves genuine nitrogen creaminess.

2. Keg Turnover & Freshness (25%)

High-volume heritage stout houses swap kegs daily or hourly, preventing stale beer sitting in lines.

3. 119.5s Two-Part Pour (20%)

Initial 45° pour, resting settle for dark ruby body development, and a slow top-off without rushing.

4. Head Density & "The Dome" (15%)

A velvety microfoam dome sitting proud of the glass rim that retains creamy lacing all the way to the bottom.

5. Proper Tulip Glassware (5%)

Spotlessly clean, dry, properly chilled official tulip glassware (never warm from a dishwasher).

6. Taste & Nitrogen Balance (5%)

Deep roasted barley warmth, smooth bitterness, and silky carbonation with no metallic taste.

Frequently Asked Questions

10 Dublin Pub Tips
1. What is the best pub crawl route in Dublin? ▼

The Classic Heritage Crawl is Dublin's most iconic pub crawl route, featuring historic pubs like Bowes, The Palace Bar, The Long Hall, Kehoe's, and Mulligan's. It covers approximately 1.8 km of walkable distance through Dublin city centre.

2. Where can you find the best pint of Guinness in Dublin? ▼

Top-rated pubs for Guinness stout quality in Dublin include John Kavanagh 'The Gravediggers' in Glasnevin, Mulligan's of Poolbeg Street, Bowes Lounge on Fleet Street, and The Long Hall on South Great George's Street, each boasting a 9.5+ Stout Score.

3. How do I do a self-guided pub crawl in Dublin? ▼

You can use the Dublin Pub Crawl Generator to customize a 3 to 7 stop route based on your preferred pub vibe (Heritage, Trad Music, Victorian Snugs, or Literary). You can export the walking itinerary directly to Google Maps or send it to your phone via QR code.

4. What are the best alternatives to Temple Bar pubs in Dublin? ▼

For authentic pub atmosphere without inflated tourist prices, explore the historic Liberties district (Fallon's, The Lord Edward, The Spire), Smithfield & Stoneybatter (The Cobblestone, Frank Ryan's), or South City Centre (Kehoe's, Neary's, Peter's Pub).

5. Where can I hear live traditional Irish music in Dublin pubs? ▼

The Cobblestone in Smithfield is world-famous for spontaneous, unamplified trad sessions 7 nights a week. Other authentic music pubs include O'Donoghue's on Merrion Row, The Piper's Corner on Marlborough Street, and Devitts of Camden Street.

6. Do Dublin pubs accept credit cards or do I need cash? ▼

Almost all Dublin pubs accept contactless, debit, and credit cards (Visa and Mastercard), including Apple Pay and Google Pay. However, carrying €20–€30 in cash is recommended for tipping musicians during trad sessions, using cloakrooms, or visiting traditional cash-only snugs.

7. What time do historic pubs close in Dublin? ▼

Under Irish licensing laws, standard closing times are 11:30 PM Monday through Thursday, 12:30 AM on Friday and Saturday, and 11:00 PM on Sunday. Heritage pubs provide 30 minutes 'drinking-up time' after last orders before patrons must vacate.

8. How much does a pint of Guinness cost in Dublin in 2026? ▼

In traditional Dublin heritage pubs outside Temple Bar, a pint of Guinness typically costs between €5.80 and €6.80. Inside tourist-heavy Temple Bar, prices frequently exceed €8.50 to €10.00, especially during late-night live music sessions.

9. What is the proper etiquette for ordering Guinness in Dublin? ▼

Always order your Guinness first before other drinks so the bartender can begin the two-part pour (which takes 119.5 seconds to settle). Never reach for or drink the pint while it is still settling and cloudy—wait until the stout is deep ruby-black with a crisp, creamy white dome.

10. Is a self-guided pub crawl safe in Dublin? ▼

Yes, Dublin city centre is very walkable and safe for self-guided pub crawls. Stick to well-lit main streets when walking between central districts (such as Grafton Street, George's Street, and Dame Street), keep your phone secure, and drink plenty of water between heritage pints.
